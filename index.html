01 — The AR rakhi

You don't send a picture of a rakhi. You tie one.

Point the camera at your brother's wrist — wherever he is standing, in a hospital corridor or a bunker or a Berlin kitchen — and the thread wraps around it in real space. He sees it on his arm. You hear the moment land. Then it stays in his gallery, in 3D, for as long as he keeps it.

Tied in AR, on the real wrist

Camera on, wrist detected, thread wraps and knots. Works on any phone browser — no headset, no app.

Your voice, not a caption

The aarti plays in your mother's actual voice. Everyone in the family adds a line and it arrives as one thread.

Kept in VR, not deleted

Every rakhi you receive stays in a private room you can walk back into next year, and the year after.

02 — How to use it

Four steps. About three minutes.

1

Open the link

No download, no account. It opens in the browser you already have.

2

Pick your rakhi

Choose a thread and record a voice message in your own language.

3

Add the family

Invite parents, partner and cousins. Each one adds their blessing to the same thread.

4

Send and tie

They raise the camera, the rakhi lands on their wrist, and it is saved forever.

03 — Why this exists

Last year I missed my sister's rakhi.

I was in another state, building the business. The work did not stop for the festival. My sister kept the rakhi on the table the whole day and we spoke for four minutes on a call at night.

That is the whole reason this exists. Not a product idea — a day I did not get back. This year I want every sibling, every soldier at a post, every parent whose child moved abroad, to be able to tie a real rakhi in AR and actually feel it happen.
